OPTION 1

Comfortable density

same rail, breathing

Variant 1 — "Comfortable density." Same information architecture as today (lenses, topics, unfiled, tags), fixed purely by giving it a real type scale and real air: 15px row labels, 11.5px uppercase section heads, 13-14px counts/chips, 40-44px hit targets, generous line-height and padding throughout. Legibility comes from that disciplined scale plus a consistent left-icon/label/count grid so the eye has a rail to travel down. Unfiled stays inside the topic list's rhythm (same row height, same position) but gets a warm accent-tinted background block, a bold count and a small "sparkle" glyph so it reads as an inviting to-do, not an error state or a stray topic.

OPTION 2

Grouped surface cards

sections as panels

Variant 2 — "Grouped surface cards." The rail is broken into distinct raised panels (Lenses / Topics / Unfiled / Tags) instead of one flat scrolling list, so the eye locates a section by its card boundary, not by parsing rows. Legibility comes from a flat size increase (15-16px row labels, 11-12px card labels, 13px chips) plus generous 34-40px hit targets and a quiet two-tone card fill. Unfiled prominence comes from being pulled out as its own physically separated card between Topics and Tags, sitting in an accent-tinted surface with an oversized rounded badge and an inviting "needs a topic" subtext — it reads as a task tray waiting to be cleared, not as an error state.

OPTION 3

Modern app sidebar

Linear / Things / Mail

Variant 3 — "Modern app sidebar" (Linear / Things / Apple Mail / Notion muscle memory). Every row is a tall pill: a fixed 20px leading column (icon or colour dot) so every label lands on the same x-position, a right-aligned muted numeral, and a selected state that is a SOLID filled pill (var(--accent) background + var(--bg) text) rather than a faint tint — this is how mail/project sidebars read "you are here" at a glance. (a) Legibility: labels are 15px, section headers 12px uppercase, tag chips 13px, every row/chip is 38-40px tall with generous horizontal padding, so trackpad hits are forgiving. (b) Unfiled prominence: it sits at the bottom of the Topics group as the one row that is ALWAYS coloured — a warm accent-tinted filled pill with an inbox icon and a badge-style count — so unlike the neutral topic rows (coloured only on hover/select) it reads as a standing invitation ("9 waiting to be filed") rather than an error state.

OPTION 4

Editorial contents page

serif table of contents

Variant 4 — "Editorial contents page". The rail reads as a magazine's table of contents: topic names sit in Source Serif 4 at 16.5px with a dotted leader running to a small mono page-count, exactly like a TOC entry running to a page number. (a) Legibility: every list label lives at 14px+ (topics at 16.5px), section heads at 11px mono, chips at 12.5px — a straight jump from the ~12px/26px-row original, plus generous 34-40px row heights. (b) Unfiled prominence: instead of styling it as one more contents line, it is pulled out as a tinted, hairline-bordered "stub" insert with its own big serif figure and a quiet "File now" action — visually an editorial call-out, not a to-do that reads as an error. Selection stays calm (accent margin bar + weight shift, no fills) so the one filled insert — Unfiled — is the page's clear focal point; hover states get a warm surface wash to keep the page from feeling inert.

OPTION 5

Topics first

everything else earns its place

Variant 5 — "Topics first, everything else earns its place" Idea: topics are the whole point of this rail, so they get the whole rail. Header collapses to one line, the three lenses become a compact icon-button trio under search, and TAGS becomes a disclosure sitting under the topics (open here, but visually second-class: smaller type, muted surface). (a) Legibility: topic rows are 44px tall with 17-18px labels and mono-figure counts big enough to scan at a glance without aiming. (b) Unfiled prominence: it sits above the topic list as its own accent-tinted block with a left accent bar, an inbox icon instead of a topic dot (so it reads as "not a topic"), an oversized count, and a one-word "Sort →" invitation — loud, but framed as an easy win, not an error.
